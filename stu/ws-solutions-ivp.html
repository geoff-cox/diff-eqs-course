    Worksheet 1.3 Verifying Solutions & Initial Conditions
    

    🎯 Learning Objectives

    • Verify whether a given function satisfies a differential equation.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Distinguish a general solution, a particular solution, and a family of solutions.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Apply initial conditions to a general solution to produce the particular solution of an initial-value problem.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Match the number of initial conditions to the number of arbitrary constants a general solution carries.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    What does it mean to solve a differential equation?
    🔗
    In algebra, a number satisfies an equation when substituting it makes both sides equal. A differential equation is satisfied by a function: substitute the function and its derivatives, simplify, and see whether the two sides become the very same expression.
    🔗
    Reading check. Fill in each blank from your reading.
    1. A solution to a differential equation is a that satisfies the equation, and “satisfies” means both sides simplify to the expression.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. To verify a proposed solution: compute any needed , them into the equation, and simplify both sides.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    1. True or False.

    To verify that a function is a solution, you must first solve the differential equation.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Consider the differential equation \(w' + 4w = 0\text{.}\) Verify that \(w = e^{-4t}\) is a solution and that \(w = e^{4t}\) is not.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    (a) Verify that \(Q = \cos(3t)\) is a solution of
    \begin{equation*} Q'' + 9Q = 0\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    (b) Without any new computation, explain why the same function is not a solution of
    \begin{equation*} Q'' + Q' + 9Q = 0\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Being a solution is a relationship between a function and a specific equation.
    🔗
    🔗
    One solution or many? General, particular, family.
    🔗
    Differential equations almost never have just one solution. Arbitrary constants let one formula describe every solution at once, so we need names to keep the ideas straight.
    🔗
    Reading check.
    1. A general solution is a formula containing one or more arbitrary ; assigning each of them a specific value produces a solution.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. The family of solutions is the of all particular solutions.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    4.

    For \(t > 0\text{,}\) the differential equation \(t\,y' + 2y = 10\) has general solution
    \begin{equation*} y = 5 + \dfrac{c}{t^2}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗

    (a)

    Verify the general solution.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Write down two different particular solutions.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    One of \(y = 5 - \dfrac{1}{t^2}\) and \(y = 3 + \dfrac{1}{t^2}\) belongs to the family of solutions and one does not. Which is which? Justify both answers.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5. True or False.

    Any solution that contains an arbitrary constant is a general solution.
    🔗
    🔗

    6.

    Find all values of \(m\) for which \(y = e^{mt}\) is a solution of \(y'' - y' - 6y = 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Initial conditions pin down the constants.
    🔗
    A general solution is a menu of infinitely many functions. Real problems come with known information (e.g., a starting height, a launch velocity) that selects exactly one item off the menu.
    🔗
    Reading check.
    1. An initial condition is a known value of the variable, or of one of its , at a specific input.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. A differential equation together with initial conditions is an initial-value (IVP), and solving it produces a solution.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. To determine every constant, the number of initial conditions must match the number of arbitrary in the general solution.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    7. True or False.

    The equation \(y' = x + y\) is an initial-value problem.
    🔗
    🔗

    8.

    A small object falls through the air, and its downward velocity \(v(t)\) (in \(\mathrm{m/s}\) ) is modeled by the IVP
    \begin{gather*} v' = 8 - 2v, \qquad v(0) = 1. \end{gather*}
    (a) Verify that \(v = 4 + ce^{-2t}\) is a solution of the differential equation for every constant \(c\text{.}\) (b) Apply the initial condition to find the particular solution. (c) What happens to the velocity as \(t \to \infty\text{?}\) Interpret your answer physically.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    9.

    The general solution of \(y'' + 3y' - 10y = 0\) is \(y = c_1 e^{2t} + c_2 e^{-5t}\text{.}\) Find the particular solution satisfying
    \begin{gather*} y(0) = 1, \qquad y'(0) = 9. \end{gather*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    10.

    Initial conditions need not be at zero. For \(t > 0\text{,}\) the general solution of \(t\,y'' + y' = 0\) is \(y = c_1 + c_2 \ln t\text{.}\) Find the particular solution satisfying
    \begin{gather*} y(1) = 2, \qquad y'(1) = 3. \end{gather*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Looking ahead. Everything on this worksheet started from a solution someone handed us. The rest of the course builds the machinery that produces those solutions. We begin with the simplest case: \(y' = f(t)\text{,}\) solved by integration where the arbitrary constant of a general solution is exactly the \(+C\) you had to write in Calculus.
